01 · About

About Benj & His Keynotes

Who is Benj Gershman?

Benj Gershman is the multi-platinum original bassist of O.A.R., a keynote speaker, podcaster, and founder of Healthy Talks LLC, based in Encinitas, California.

He draws on over three decades on stage — including his TEDxVailYouth talk Mental Resilience is the New Seatbelt and his DisruptHR San Diego talk The Band Effect — to help corporate teams, leaders, and student audiences build resilience, work in harmony, and perform under pressure. He also hosts the What Could Be Bad podcast.

What are your most-requested keynote topics?

Two keynotes anchor Benj's work:

Mental Resilience is the New Seatbelt reframes mental resilience as a daily, learnable practice — the kind of thing you don't think about until you need it. Drawn directly from his TEDxVailYouth talk.

The Band Effect: How Great Teams Create Harmony uses the three-part framework Tune In, Turn Up, Rock It to show how high-performing teams build and sustain real alignment under pressure.

Both can be delivered as standalones, blended into a single talk, or adapted for student and youth audiences.

What makes your keynotes different from other speakers?

Three things.

First, Benj brings over three decades on stage — including as the multi-platinum original bassist of O.A.R. — not borrowed credibility.

Second, the music isn't a prop — he DJs and layers tracks live, so the audience hears the framework being built in real time, not watching a slide deck.

Third, the content is grounded in stoic philosophy and peer-reviewed research on anxiety, resilience, and workplace mental-health ROI — not just personal anecdotes.

02 · The Experience

The Audience & The Experience

Who do you speak to — executives, employees, students, or all three?

All three. Benj tailors every keynote to the room: corporate HR and leadership teams at sales kickoffs (SKOs), all-hands, offsites, and HR conferences; student-leadership audiences and college-conference attendees; and mission-driven organizations in healthcare, wellness, and education.

The core frameworks stay the same — the stories, examples, and language shift to fit the audience in the room.

Will my team walk away with practical tools, or is this just inspiration?

Both. Every keynote hands the audience a clear framework — the Seatbelt metaphor for resilience, or Tune In / Turn Up / Rock It for team harmony — plus specific habits and language they can use the next day.

Benj also offers follow-on workshops (Seatbelts & Toolkits, Presence & Influence, and Intellectual Athletes) when a client wants the keynote to turn into sustained behavior change rather than a one-day lift.

What's the tone — is it heavy or energizing?

Energizing. The material touches on real topics — anxiety, burnout, the quiet pressure most high performers carry — but the delivery is warm, funny, and full of music. Audiences leave fired up, not weighed down.

Benj's signature close, used across every keynote, is: "Be good to yourself. Be good to others."

Do you speak to youth or student audiences?

Yes. Benj delivers a dedicated version of his keynote for college-leadership conferences, student organizations, and high-school leadership programs. It merges the resilience and team-harmony material from his corporate talks with music the students help build in the room.

His TEDxVailYouth talk, Mental Resilience is the New Seatbelt, is the origin point of this work and remains a highly-requested format at student retreats and leadership summits.

03 · Working Together

Working Together

Can you customize the keynote to our organization or event theme?

Yes — and most clients ask for it. Benj runs a pre-event intake with the organizer and, when it's useful, interviews two to four members of your team to gather the stories and language that make the keynote feel like it was written for your event.

The frameworks don't change; the examples, the music, and the specific language do.

How do you prepare for an event?

A typical prep arc looks like this:

One or two intake calls with the organizer; a short discovery questionnaire; optional interviews with two to four members of your team to source stories; and a technical walk-through with your A/V team ahead of the day.

For co-branded or themed events, Benj also tailors the walk-on music, the DJ moments, and the examples he uses on stage.

Do you deliver virtually, in person, or both?

Both. In-person is Benj's default — the music and energy carry differently in a room — but virtual keynotes and hybrid formats are available, with the same interactive moments adapted for a camera and a chat window.

What audience sizes work best?

Benj has worked rooms from 25-person leadership offsites to 2,000+ general sessions, plus virtual gatherings in the thousands. The format scales — small rooms get more interaction and participation; large rooms get more production and spectacle.

Do you ever co-present with other speakers?

Yes. Benj co-presents a fireside-chat program, From the Stage to the Office, with workplace mental-health expert Melissa Doman, M.A. — a shared-stage format designed for HR and leadership audiences that pairs lived experience with peer-reviewed research.
